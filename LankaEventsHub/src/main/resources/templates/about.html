Our story

One system, instead
of two that disagree.

Most event tools serve either the organizer or the guest. Running the event in one system and selling seats in another is how a venue ends up double-booked, how a seat gets sold twice, and how somebody arrives holding a ticket for an evening that moved last week.

Verdant Events keeps both sides in one place. The capacity an organizer sets is the capacity a guest sees. The seat a guest books is the seat the door expects. Payment is what confirms a booking, so a confirmed booking always means somebody paid. There is no second copy of the truth to drift out of step with the first.

Every rule in the platform earns its place by preventing something that actually goes wrong on the night — not by being a feature worth listing.

What we hold to

Four principles.

01

Say the true number

Seats, prices and availability are read from the database when the page loads. If an event has four seats left, it says four.

02

Nothing sold twice

Two people clicking for the last seat at the same moment cannot both succeed. The database enforces it, not the form.

03

Confirmed means paid

A booking is confirmed by money arriving — at checkout or at the door. Nothing else promotes it.

04

Everything traceable

Approvals, cancellations, refunds and deletions are recorded with who did them and what went — before it goes.
